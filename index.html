    The problem

    Agents now act. Accountability has not caught up.

    AI has moved from answering questions to taking actions: paying invoices, moving data, changing records. Each step up in autonomy multiplies the actions taken without a person, while the number of people available to review them stays the same.

    Why this matters on the path to AGI →

    One platform

    Discover. Govern. Enforce. Detect. Explain.

    Five functions in one system, so the record of what an agent was allowed to do and the record of what it did are the same record.

    01

    Discover

    What AI is acting in our name?

    Builds a live inventory of the agents and AI-enabled tools operating across the organisation, including AI inside vendor software, with an owner for each.

    02

    Govern

    What may each agent do?

    Turns policy, delegation maps, trust deeds and mandates into machine-readable rules: what an agent may execute, what it may only recommend, and what it must never touch.

    03

    Enforce

    Is this action within the mandate?

    The Accountability Guard sits between agents and the systems they act on. Every action is checked before it executes: allowed, held for a named person, or blocked.

    04

    Detect

    Is something going wrong?

    Watches behaviour across agents for patterns no single rule would catch: repeated near-limit actions, unusual timing, attempts to widen scope, and signs of manipulated instructions.

    05

    Explain

    Who decided, on what basis?

    Every decision, including every refusal, produces a record a trustee, auditor, board or regulator can read: the action, the rule applied, the outcome and the accountable person.

    What Detect and Explain reveal feeds back into Govern, so mandates improve with use.

    How it was built

    From brain-inspired research to a runtime for accountability

    HyperMindQ grew out of research into brain-inspired, event-driven computing, and from advisory work on how organisations should delegate decisions to AI. Spiking neural networks respond to each event as it happens. The Guard applies the same principle to oversight: every action is checked when it occurs, not in a review weeks later.
