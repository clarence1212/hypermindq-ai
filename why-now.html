Why now

The path to AGI runs through accountability

Whether general intelligence arrives in five years or twenty is debated. The direction is not: more actions taken by AI, faster than people can review them. Accountability has to be built into the systems themselves.

The autonomy ladder

Six steps from tools to superintelligence

Each step moves more decisions from people to systems. Most organisations are now at step two, moving to three.

L0

Tools

Software that does exactly what it is told. People take every decision.

L1

Copilots

AI that drafts, summarises and suggests. A person reviews each output.

L2

Agents

AI that takes actions in systems: payments, records, messages. A person sets the task and checks some results.

L3

Multi-agent systems

Agents that delegate to other agents across organisations. No single person sees every step.

L4

General intelligence

Systems able to pursue open-ended goals across domains, at machine speed.

L5

Superintelligence

Systems more capable than people in most domains. Oversight must be built in, because it cannot be added afterwards.

Why oversight must move into the runtime

Six reasons review after the fact is no longer enough

Speed

Agents act in milliseconds. A monthly review, or even a daily one, finds problems after the money has moved.

Scale

One person can review a handful of decisions an hour. A fleet of agents can take thousands.

Opacity

When agents delegate to other agents, no one sees the full chain unless it is recorded as it happens.

Manipulation

Agents read emails, documents and web pages. Instructions hidden in that content can redirect them.

Responsibility does not move

Directors, trustees and officers remain accountable for what is done in their name, whoever or whatever does it.

Capability will keep rising

Whatever the timeline to AGI, controls designed for today’s agents must hold for tomorrow’s. They have to be structural, not optional.

The principle

Capability may be delegated. Accountability may not.

As systems become more capable, the temptation is to trust them more and check them less. The safer course is the reverse: the more an agent can do, the more precisely its mandate should be defined, enforced and recorded.

That is what HyperMindQ is for. It does not decide what an organisation should delegate. People decide that. It makes sure the decision holds, every time an agent acts, and that someone can always explain what happened.

Guidance we align with

Published frameworks point the same way

HyperMindQ is designed around themes common to published guidance. It is not certified or endorsed by these bodies, and using it does not by itself demonstrate compliance.

  • Model AI Governance Framework for Agentic AI ↗22 January 2026
    IMDA, Singapore

    Assess and bound risks upfront, keep humans meaningfully accountable, apply technical controls across the agent lifecycle, and enable end-user responsibility.

  • Guidelines on Artificial Intelligence Risk Management ↗Consultation 13 November 2025; response published 7 October 2026
    Monetary Authority of Singapore

    Board and senior management oversight, AI inventories, risk materiality, life-cycle controls and third-party AI for financial institutions.

  • Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, the AI Act: Article 14, human oversight ↗Adopted 13 June 2024
    European Union

    High-risk AI systems must be designed so that people can effectively oversee them, understand their limits, and intervene or stop them.
